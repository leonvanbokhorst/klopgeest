## 1. Answer

The instrument can carry a light signal today without any new DSP architecture, and the research
needed to implement it now exists in a form a builder can code from. The answer has four parts.

**(a) The signal is well-defined enough to implement, and the one thing most likely to be got wrong
is the brightness ratio.** SpectraStrobe is four high-frequency sine carriers in a stereo file:
18.2 kHz reference, 18.7 red, 19.2 green, 19.7 blue. A colour's brightness is its carrier amplitude
**divided by the reference's**. The format's own author and its principal developer state, four years
apart, that full brightness sits **+12 dB above the reference** (reference −36 dBFS, colour
−24 dBFS) — a **4:1** ratio. One third-party mixing table implies 1:1; every numeric artefact
produced by the people who make the format sits at +12 to +14.5 dB. **Adopt 4:1.** Implementing 1:1
under-drives every colour by 12 dB, and there is a plausible link to the one real hardware session
that reported "no convincing green or yellow".

**(b) The architecture is decided by one existing component.** KLOPGEEST's master bus is a
compressor, a saturator and a zero-look-ahead limiter ending in a literal clamp at a ceiling that can
reach 0 dBFS. Carriers that enter it are gain-ridden by the drum peaks and clipped flat — and a
clipped 19 kHz sine splatters harmonics across the band. **The carriers must be added after
`master.process` and after the meter loop; nothing may follow them.** Everything else follows from
that: a `LightEngine` living inside `Engine::process`, reading the `SequencedEvent` array that is
already sample-accurate, already carrying groove deviation, and already in scope at that point.

**(c) The musical design question has an answer, and it is a constraint disguised as a limitation.**
Light becomes a rhythmic representation rather than a visualisation when it is generated by the same
decision layer as the sound but given different laws: its own envelopes, its own memory, its own
assignment of what matters, and the freedom to not respond. Concretely: **luminance is slow; colour
and position carry the detail.** Three independent forces point the same way — the format's 500 Hz
channel spacing punishes fast envelope edges with colour crosstalk, the photosensitivity evidence
forbids 3–30 Hz luminance modulation, and the issue itself asks that light not flash on every event.
The result is a rule that is both safe and musically interesting: **light follows the beat, never the
subdivision**, and at fast tempi it subdivides *slower*. A track speeding from 120 to 200 BPM visibly
calms its light. That is the opposite of what a visualiser does, and it is the clearest demonstration
that the light is composed.

Three mapping personalities with concrete numbers are proposed: **PULSE** (brightness carries the
rhythm — the skeleton breathes), **COUNTERPOINT** (colour and space carry it — the light has its own
metre and repeats every 70 s where the audio repeats in 2 s), and **DRIFT** (time
carries it — the light lags, holds a memory, and changes character on mutation rather than adding
flashes). Two macros, INTENSITY and COUPLING, as the issue asked.

**(d) There is a real, unresolved tension that a builder must not paper over: conservative ≠ usable.**
The inherited conservative cap is **`r` = 0.25** — colour at 25 % of the *reference amplitude*, which
is **6.25 % of format full brightness** ([`01`](spectrastrobe/01-signal-spec.md) §3 defines the units
and the pack uses them everywhere). When a predecessor project played a rigorously verified file at
**`r` = 0.0625** (1.56 % of full brightness) on a real Kasina, the observer saw red, blue and violet but **no
convincing green or yellow**, and crossfades read as **fading to black** — although the file had
0.0 ms of encoded black gap. Nobody established a cause. The conservative default may simply be below
the decoder's useful floor. That question is answerable only by the hardware ladder in
[`06-hardware-validation.md`](spectrastrobe/06-hardware-validation.md) §3, and until it is run, the
honest position is: **ship conservative, say the light may be dim, and do not raise the ceiling to
compensate before the ratio question is settled.**

**What this research could not do:** establish that any of it works as *art*. Criterion C5/D8 —
whether a person seeing only the light can tell the mappings apart and tell a tight take from a feral
one — needs a build, a Kasina and a person. Nothing in this pack substitutes for it, and no claim that
the mappings "work" should be made before it is answered.

## 2. Status of the propositions

Vocabulary: **established** (in a retrieved primary source, or read directly in the code),
**contested** (two sources disagree; both quoted), **provisional** (inference or design choice, not
yet tested), **not established** (outside the evidence), **not knowable with these methods**.

### The signal

| Proposition | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Carriers at 18.2 / 18.7 / 19.2 / 19.7 kHz | **Established** | Four independent witnesses, including the format owner's spec and two implementations' source. |
| Green 19.2 kHz is also the AudioStrobe carrier, and losing the reference degrades to AudioStrobe | **Established** | Kasina documentation + encoder author. |
| Reference = 20 Hz hard L/R pan, 100 % depth, 0 % offset; 10–50 Hz permitted; sine recommended | **Established** | Vendor spec. |
| Reference waveform/duty: sine vs 50 %-duty pulse vs 12.5 % rectangular | **Contested** | All documented; decoder described as "quite tolerant". We choose sine. |
| The reference is a *scale*, not only a flag | **Established** | Encoder author ("sets the dynamic range … 4:1") and a moderator ("lower reference ⇒ brighter colours"). |
| Full-brightness colour = 4 × reference (+12 dB) | **Contested, resolved for engineering** | Two independent developer statements + their own tooling at +12…+14.5 dB, versus one third-party table at 1:1. See §4. |
| The decoder's level→brightness transfer law | **Not established** | Never published. The ratio model is an inference that fits all sources. |
| Formats: stereo, 44.1/48 kHz, 16-bit, dither off, no metadata, no Wave64; MP3 320 CBR stereo only | **Established** | Vendor + MindPlace documentation. |
| Worst-case coherent carrier sum ≈ −14 dBFS at full brightness | **Provisional** (arithmetic) | 3 colours at −24 dBFS + reference at −36 dBFS = 0.205 → −13.8 dBFS. |
| Kasina: six light channels (RGB × both eyes), independent LED brightness, per-colour/per-eye balance | **Established** | Kasina manual + vendor device docs. |
| Decoder filter topology, detection thresholds, autosense timing, stop behaviour, absolute luminance | **Not established** | Publicly undocumented; hardware measurement only. |

### The design

| Proposition | Status |
|---|---|
| 4:1 is the correct operating point | **Contested, resolved provisionally** — best-supported reading; one hardware test settles it. |
| Luminance must stay below 3 Hz; colour/position can carry detail | **Provisional, well-founded** — WCAG 2.3.1 is established; that a Ganzfeld needs a stricter margin is inference. |

**Primary sources for the instrument:** the KLOPGEEST source tree itself, read directly, with a

**The predecessor implementation:** NAGLOED branch `feature/v2-exploration`, commit `862dec2`. Audited

**Evidence quality note.** Repeated publication is not corroboration here. The Lumasonic documentation

Lumasonic docs. The genuinely independent witnesses for the carrier frequencies are the MindPlace
tooling and SpiraStrober; for the ratio, the two MindPlace-side developers plus their tooling, against
one vendor table.

## 4. Counter-evidence and tensions

A report with no tensions would be a report that did not look. These remain open.

**T1 — The ratio conflict is genuine, not settled by assertion.** The Lumasonic production/mixing
table says a colour track at reference level is full brightness (1:1). The engineering resolution in
§1(a) is a **model**, not a quotation: it fits every statement including the awkward ones (why
lowering the reference brightens the colours; why a user who "matched colour to reference" got
proportional but not maximum brightness), but the decoder's transfer function is unpublished. If
hardware testing shows green saturating at a lower level than red, the model is wrong and every
colour is over-driven by 12 dB. This is why [`01`](spectrastrobe/01-signal-spec.md) recommends a
single `kFullScaleRatio` constant and independent Reference/Colour gain trims: the fix is a parameter,
not a rewrite.

**T2 — The one hardware observation contradicts the optimistic reading of colour.** A rigorously
verified file, at `r` = 0.0625 per carrier on a −30 dBFS reference, produced red, blue and violet but **no
convincing green or yellow**, and crossfades that read as black. Three explanations are compatible
with the evidence and were never separated: (i) the level is simply below a threshold — which the
4:1 model makes very plausible, since `r` = 0.0625 is **1.56 % of full brightness** under it;
(ii) the hardware or the diffuser has a per-colour imbalance; (iii) observer/perceptual effects. The
counter-evidence to the report's own optimism is real, and the design response is to **not encode
meaning in colour identity**, to **make before break** on every transition, and to **allow a
per-colour trim**.

**T3 — Conservative and usable may be in direct conflict.** The cap `r` = 0.25 against a −36 dBFS
reference is −48.04 dBFS per colour. Under the 4:1 model that is roughly 6 % of full brightness. The safety
literature's thresholds were written for screens with distance and a small stimulus area; a Ganzfeld
removes both. So the conservative setting is conservative relative to a number that is itself a lower
bound for a different geometry. There is no measured answer to "how bright is safe", and the report
does not invent one. The position taken: ship low, say it may be dim, let the hardware ladder find the
usable floor, and treat any raised ceiling as a safety decision requiring its own review.

**T4 — "Slow light" and "rhythmic light" pull against each other.** The whole design rests on making
luminance slow. But if light is too slow, it stops being rhythmic and becomes ambient. The report's
answer is that rhythm moves into colour, overlap and space — but that is a **hypothesis** (C5), and it
is entirely possible that a viewer experiences PULSE and COUNTERPOINT as "the same glow, different
colours". If so the design has failed and needs rethinking, not retuning.

**T5 — "Parallel voice" and "the audio is untouched" are not the same claim.** Criterion C1 was
originally written as one bit-identity test and that was wrong: the programme filter and the headroom
gain are real, declared changes, and a bit-identity gate would be impossible by construction. C1 is
now split — C1a (light off, bit-identical), C1b (light on, music structurally unchanged), C1c (the
filter does its job and nothing else). See [`05`](spectrastrobe/05-acceptance-and-tests.md) §4.
The cleanest form of C1b would be if nothing touched the programme at all. But the format requires keeping
programme energy out of 18–20 kHz, and KLOPGEEST's hats and claps are broadband. The report prefers
four narrow notches over a full low-pass precisely because notches leave the audible band
untouched — but that is an untested idea, not vendor guidance. If it does not work, C1 weakens to
"identical modulo a documented filter", which is a materially worse test.

**T6 — The inherited research is not an independent witness.** Much of the safety framing and several
of the signal claims come from a project by the same author. Where the two research streams agree, that
is not corroboration. The one place this mattered was caught and corrected: the inherited `r` = 0.25
convention had been documented as applying to a −30 dBFS reference in some places and −36 dBFS in
others, with the A/B protocol's own text initially mis-classifying which ladder steps sat above the
ceiling — corrected by the maintainer on 5 November, and carried into this report as
[`01`](spectrastrobe/01-signal-spec.md) §3 with the −36 dBFS reference as the operating point.

**T7 — A prior research error was found in the inherited verification tooling.** The M5 LFO
demodulator cannot separate carriers 500 Hz apart: measured, a 25 % colour pulls the "off-phase
rejection" metric to −21.5 dB and would **fail a perfect signal**. The M3 ratio denominator zero-pads
past end-of-file and reported a 25 % colour as **663 %** on a 2 s vector. Both are documented in
[`05`](spectrastrobe/05-acceptance-and-tests.md) §2.3 with the corrected conventions, and the
corrected demodulator exists in that project's A/B verifier. Anyone porting the tooling must take the
correction, not the original.

## 7. Important uncertainties and limits

**Not established by this research:**

1. **Whether the light works as art.** Criterion C5/D8. Needs a build, a Kasina and a person.
2. **The decoder's transfer function.** Everything about brightness ratios rests on a model.
3. **Whether the `r` = 0.25 cap is usable.** The prior session (at `r` = 0.0625) hints it may not be.
4. **Whether -36 dBFS reference / -24 dBFS colour is right for *this* hardware and firmware.** It is the
   best-supported reading, not a measurement.
5. **Whether the four-notch alternative to low-passing the programme works.** It is an idea chosen
   because it preserves the audible band, not a documented technique.
6. **Whether a user's host chain preserves 18–20 kHz.** Host- and user-dependent, unmeasured.
7. **Whether the carriers are audible at the delivered level.** Depends on age, hearing and
   transducers. Documented as "usually inaudible, not to everyone", never as "inaudible".
8. **Any absolute safety claim.** Every published threshold is for screens; nothing here measured a
   Ganzfeld in photometric units.

**Limits of the method:**

- One hardware session exists in the whole evidence base, from a different instrument, with no
  photodiode and no time-synchronised observation. Treat it as a single qualitative report.
- The inherited research shares an author with this project. Agreement between the two is not
  independent corroboration (T6).
- The golden vectors' expected values are measured **on this machine** and depend on CPython's
  `round()` and the system libm. They are a per-platform oracle, not a cross-machine hash contract.
- The design in [`03`](spectrastrobe/03-light-engine-design.md) is a proposal with numbers. Not one
  envelope time, colour or depth in it has been heard or seen.
- This report did not fetch the predecessor project's GitHub issues (`#14`, `#31`); it read the
  checked-in research documents instead. That is a gap in the trail, not a conflicting source.

## 8. What would change the answer

| If this turns out to be true | Then |
|---|---|
| Hardware shows 4:1 is correct and `r` = 0.25 is usable | The conservative defaults stand; ship. |
| Hardware shows 1:1 is correct | Every colour is currently 12 dB hot. Change one constant; re-run the ladder; the architecture is unaffected. |
| Hardware shows the useful floor is above `r` = 0.25 | The cap needs a formal safety review, not a parameter bump. The tension in T3 becomes the central design problem. |
| Green is systematically weak at any level | Per-colour balance becomes a required parameter and PULSE/COUNTERPOINT must not rely on colour identity (already the design). |
| A viewer cannot distinguish PULSE from COUNTERPOINT | The mapping design has failed. Rethink what carries the rhythm; do not retune. |
| The four-notch approach does not keep the programme out of 18–20 kHz | Fall back to a steep low-pass, accept that C1 weakens, and document the audible trade-off. |
| A host is found that reliably preserves the carriers end to end | The plug-in becomes a first-class path rather than the risky one; the standalone recommendation in `04` §7 relaxes. |
| Anyone establishes a photometric safety threshold for Ganzfelds | The whole safety framing can move from "conservative convention" to a measured bound. This would be the single most valuable external input. |

## 10. Implications for KLOPGEEST

KLOPGEEST's premise is that *"a drum machine is a clock you can argue with"*, and its whole design
effort — correlated humanisation, anchor voices that stay planted while hats lean, mutation that
changes the rhythm without replacing it — goes into making the grid behave like a drummer rather than
a metronome. A light output that flashes on every hit would assert the opposite of that premise: it
would publish the grid the instrument spends its effort bending.

The interesting consequence, and the part worth pursuing: **the instrument already contains the
material for a light composition that a beat detector could never produce.** The per-lane polymeter
means a colour field whose period is 70 s when the audio's is 2 s. The
mutation history means a character that evolves while the rhythm holds. The correlated drift means a
slow, related, non-random movement that has no audio counterpart at that timescale. None of these are
visible in the sound; all of them are computable at the moment the light needs them. That is not
decoration — it is the instrument using its own knowledge to say something it does not otherwise say.

The counterweight is honest and should be stated in the manual: **KLOPGEEST works as an instrument
today, and light must remain an option that is usually off.** The single best test of whether this
feature belongs is the last question in
[`06`](spectrastrobe/06-hardware-validation.md) §5: *if the glasses had been off, would the music have
been worse?*

## 11. Adjacent questions noticed, not researched

Logged because they are real, and none of them is authorised by this mandate:

1. **Whether the light engine should be able to render offline to a file** — for the hardware session,
   for sharing a light take, for a music video. It is the obvious next feature and it changes the
   export discipline entirely (16-bit, no metadata, no dither, 320 CBR only).
2. **Whether AudioStrobe compatibility is worth a mode.** Two channels instead of six, far more
   forgiving, and it would work on hardware that does not support SpectraStrobe. Not researched.
3. **Whether a photodiode measurement rig is worth building.** It is the only route to S1–S3 in
   [`04`](spectrastrobe/04-safety-and-opt-in.md) §9, and it would turn three open questions into
   measurements. Out of scope, and the highest-value hardware investment for this feature.
4. **Whether the instrument should expose a light "record/recall" of a performance** in the same way
   it exposes mutation history — light as another thing with a memory. Design adjacent to DRIFT.
5. **Whether the legal/regulatory position of selling an instrument with a light output needs review**
   before release. Explicitly outside this mandate, and the report does not guess at it.
6. **Whether `HitFlag::mutation` being never set is a bug independent of this feature.** The UI legend
   claims it; nothing sets it. That is a small correctness gap in the existing instrument and it was
   noticed here, not researched.
7. **Whether the standalone app should offer a dedicated light output** — a second `AudioDeviceManager`
   or a routing component. New code, plausible, not researched.

## 12. Corrections made after review

Three independent review rounds found nine defects — four in the first version of the pack, and five
more in the corrections themselves. All are fixed; they are recorded rather than quietly edited, because a
builder reading the PR history should see what changed and why.

| # | Defect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The light ceiling had two definitions 12 dB apart.** `01` §3 used "25 % of *full brightness*" (colour ≈ −36 dBFS, `r` = 1.0) while `01` §4, `05` V2/V4/V5, `06` T1 and `03` used "25 % of the *reference*" (colour ≈ −48 dBFS, `r` = 0.25). A builder would have implemented one of two incompatible specs. | One canonical quantity, **`r`**, defined once in `01` §3 with `f = r/4` and dBFS alongside; `rCap = 0.25` (6.25 % of full brightness, −48.04 dBFS) is now the single product cap, and every macro, vector, ladder step and conclusion is stated in those units. |
| 2 | **The governor could not enforce its own bound.** A token bucket of 3 refilled at 3/s permits 6 transitions in one sliding second. | Replaced with exact sliding-window accounting (a 6-entry ring of sample counters — 6 transitions ⇒ ≤ 3 flashes per WCAG 2.3.1), explicit flash/transition definitions, and mandatory adversarial boundary tests. The tension between the 20 ms artistic floor and the 5 ms panic ramp is now an explicit, reasoned exception; the panic's dependence on the host continuing to call `processBlock` is now stated as a limitation with the physical stop named as the real guarantee. |
| 3 | **C1 was impossible by construction.** It promised bit-identical audio with light on, while `01` §5–6 require a programme filter and a headroom attenuation. | Split into **C1a** (light off: bit-identical), **C1b** (light on: identical *events*, music matched after removing the two declared changes), **C1c** (the filter does its job and nothing else). The headroom rule also changed: not a fixed −6 dBTP but computed from the carrier cap in force, so the attenuation at the product cap is **0.9 dB** rather than 5.6 dB. |
| 4 | **The polymeter period was wrong.** 560 sixteenth-note steps at 120 BPM was given as "four and a half minutes". | It is **70 seconds** (560 × 0.125 s). Both the design document and the summary now say so, with the scaling stated honestly rather than rounded into rhetoric. |

A second review round on the corrected pack found two more, both in the fix itself:

| # | Defect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **The corrected sliding-window boundary cases were themselves wrong.** The rule is "at most 6 transitions in every 1 s window", under which **6 transitions inside 0.999 s passes** — the boundary table said it must fail. A compliant governor would have been failed by the pack's own acceptance gate. | Boundary cases corrected and split by rule: 6-in-0.999 s passes, 7-in-0.999 s fails, 7 spanning 1.2 s passes, 7 in `[0, 1.0]` inclusive fails. Window semantics are now stated — **closed at both ends, one second long, i.e. `fs` sample *periods* / `fs + 1` sample *instants***, so `n − t = fs` rejects and `fs + 1` admits — and the seven cases are unit tests on the admission predicate, because end-to-end tests cannot separate the two governor rules. |
| 6 | **The 2.5 Hz design rate was asserted but not enforced.** Rule R1 bounds the *count* of transitions, not their *rate*: a **2.75 Hz square wave** passes "≤ 3 flashes/s" — at most 6 transitions in any closed 1 s window — while breaking the stated ≤ 2.5 Hz limit. One merged sentence guaranteed neither. | Split into **K2a / B2** (flash count, governor rule R1) and **K2b / B2b** (pulse rate, governor rule R2: consecutive same-direction transitions ≥ 400 ms). Both are now governor rules with stated boundary behaviour, and both documents say plainly that they bound a *luminance proxy*, not photons. |
| 7 | **The C1b oracle was numerically unsound** (raised as optional; it is a real defect). It inverse-filtered the light-on render, which amplifies noise at a notch's deep stopband and makes any failure uninterpretable. | Compare **forward with forward**: apply the same documented filter and gain to the *light-off* render and compare that against the light-on render. |

A third round found two example-level errors, both in the second round's fixes:

| # | Defect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | **The counterexample contradicted the boundary convention that had just been adopted.** "3 Hz passes R1" is not universally true: with a closed 1 s window, a 3.0 Hz square has transitions every 1/6 s, so **seven fall inside `[0, 1]`** and R1 rejects it too. At 3 Hz both rules bind, so the example demonstrated nothing about their independence. | **2.75 Hz** is the correct counterexample: 5.5 transitions/s, at most 6 in any closed window (R1 passes), same-direction spacing **364 ms** (R2 fails). Stated in `03` §5.1, `04` K2 and `05` §3, with the 3 Hz case retained explicitly as a non-discriminator. |
| 9 | **The window was described as "exactly `fs` samples", which is off by one.** A closed interval of one second contains **`fs` sample periods** and **`fs + 1` sample instants**. | Reworded everywhere; the admission predicate `n − t > fs` is unchanged and correct. Noted as exactly where this class of bug lives. |

None of the nine changes a conclusion about the format, the hardware evidence, or the architecture.
They changed what the documents *say*, which is the part a builder implements.

The pattern in defects 5, 6, 8 and 9 is worth naming, because it is the general failure mode of this
kind of document: **the correction reproduced the original mistake one level down.** Fixing a wrong number
introduced a wrong boundary case; separating flash count from flash rate produced a single sentence
that enforced neither; adopting a closed window made the very next example contradict it. Each was
caught only because someone read the *rules* rather than the conclusions. A builder should expect the same to happen in the implementation, and should write the
boundary tests in [`05`](spectrastrobe/05-acceptance-and-tests.md) §3 before writing the governor.
