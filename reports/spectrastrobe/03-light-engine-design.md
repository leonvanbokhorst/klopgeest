# 03 — The light engine: mappings, macros and the answer to the design question

**The question under this document** (issue #6):

> *Hoe kan licht de ritmische identiteit, spanning en beweging van KLOPGEEST representeren zonder
> louter een beat-indicator te worden?*

This document is a design proposal with concrete numbers. Every design decision below is marked
**[C]** (a choice this project makes, retunable), **[I]** (forced by arithmetic or physics) or
**[E]**/**[O]** (established / open in the sources — see [`01-signal-spec.md`](01-signal-spec.md)).
**Nothing here has been built or tested on hardware.** The perceptual claims are hypotheses with a
stated test, not findings.

---

## 1. The answer, stated plainly

**Light becomes a rhythmic representation rather than a visualisation when it is generated by the
same musical decision layer as the sound but given different expressive laws: its own envelope times,
its own memory, its own assignment of which events matter — and the freedom to not respond.**

A visualisation is a *function of the audio*. A second voice is a *sibling of the audio*: same parent
(the groove), different instrument. Everything below is an attempt to make that distinction
architectural rather than rhetorical, and §7 turns it into five falsifiable tests.

### 1.1 The one structural rule that makes it work

SpectraStrobe's physics and light-safety rules point at the same constraint, which is a gift rather
than a limitation:

> **Luminance is slow. Colour and position are where the detail lives.**

Three independent reasons:

1. **Signal.** A modulation edge of rise time *t* spreads the carrier by ≈ *1/t* Hz, and the carriers
   are only 500 Hz apart ([`01-signal-spec.md`](01-signal-spec.md) §7). Fast brightness edges leak
   between colour channels. **[I]**
2. **Safety.** Luminance modulation between 3 and 30 Hz is the photosensitive trigger band; no more
   than three opposing 10 % luminance changes per second is the WCAG level-A threshold
   ([`04-safety-and-opt-in.md`](04-safety-and-opt-in.md) §2). **[E]**
3. **Art.** A drum machine that flashes on every hit is a metronome with more LEDs. The issue asks
   for the opposite: *"Licht hoeft niet bij ieder event te flitsen."*

So the engine is deliberately asymmetrical: **brightness carries the slow ground (breath, tension,
presence); hue and spatial position carry the fast musical detail (ghosts, ratchets, accents,
polymeter, groove deviation).** The eye reads hue and position changes continuously without a
luminance flash, which is exactly the register the drum events live in.

### 1.2 The consequence: light follows the beat, never the subdivision

Because luminance edges are rate-limited, light cannot track 16th notes. At 120 BPM a 16th is 8 Hz; at
200 BPM, 13.3 Hz. Both are inside the danger band and both are musically pointless.

**Rule [C]:** the light's fundamental luminance period is the beat, or a **sub-harmonic** of it,
chosen so the pulse rate never exceeds **2.5 Hz**:

| Tempo | Beat rate | Light pulse divisor | Resulting pulse |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 BPM | 1.50 Hz | ×1 | 1.50 Hz |
| 120 BPM | 2.00 Hz | ×1 | 2.00 Hz |
| 160 BPM | 2.67 Hz | ×2 | 1.33 Hz |
| 200 BPM | 3.33 Hz | ×2 | 1.67 Hz |

**This is a musical feature, not a compromise.** At fast tempi the light automatically breathes
slower than the music — it gains weight, it stops being a tempo display, it becomes an accompaniment.
A track that speeds up from 120 to 200 BPM visibly calms its light. That is the opposite of what a
visualiser does, and it is the single clearest demonstration that the light is composed rather than
derived.

## 2. What the light engine is made of

### 2.1 Data flow

```
                    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
SequencedEvent[] ──▶│  LIGHT ENGINE  (inside Engine::process)  │
 (voice, offset,    │                                          │
  velocity, flags,  │  1. AGGREGATE   ratchet groups → events  │
  timingDevMs,      │  2. ASSIGN      event → light voice(s)   │
  tickIndex,        │  3. ENVELOPE    6 × AD envelope, slow    │
  stepIndex)        │  4. ACCUMULATE  6 gains, hue-normalised  │
                    │  5. GOVERN      slew, luminance, edges   │
                    └──────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                       │ 6 gains ∈ [0,1] + 6 per-sample ramps
                    ┌──────────────────▼───────────────────────┐
programme ─────────▶│  ENCODER  (per sample)                   │──▶ output
 (post master)      │  ref carrier + 3 colour carriers × 2 ears │    L/R
                    └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The light engine runs at **control rate** (recommend 64 samples, 750 Hz at 48 kHz) for envelope
bookkeeping, but its six output gains are **linearly interpolated to every sample** by the encoder.
A gain held for 64 samples and then stepped is a 750 Hz modulation of a carrier 500 Hz from its
neighbour — crosstalk by construction ([`01-signal-spec.md`](01-signal-spec.md) §7).

### 2.2 The six light channels

Three colours × two eyes. Panning a colour channel moves it between eyes; it does not attenuate it
(the colour pan law keeps unity at centre — **do not use the equal-power law for colours**, it costs
3 dB of decoded brightness at centre). The reference carrier uses equal-power pan; the two laws are
different on purpose ([`evidence/reference-implementation.md`](evidence/reference-implementation.md) §2).

### 2.3 A light voice

A light voice is the unit of composition. Six available; a mapping uses two to four.

```cpp
struct LightVoiceParams {
    uint8_t  sourceMask;       // bit i = drum voice i contributes
    float    sourceWeight[8];  // 0..1 per drum voice
    float    colour[3];        // r,g,b target, normalised so max(c)=1
    float    attackMs;         // >= 20 (HARD), default per voice
    float    releaseMs;        // >= 50 for artistic events
    float    depth;            // 0..1 contribution to the channel ceiling
    float    velocityCurve;    // 0 = linear, 1 = sqrt (lifts ghosts)
    EyeMode  eye;              // both | left | right | alternate
    SourceMode mode;           // EVENT | INTEGRATE | PHASE | DRIFT
    float    delayMs;          // 0..300, "memory"
    bool     accentOnly;       // respond only to HitFlag::accent
};
```

| `SourceMode` | Behaviour | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| `EVENT` | One envelope per (aggregated) event. | bounded by governor |
| `INTEGRATE` | One-pole follower over the sources' velocity stream. **Produces no discrete edges.** | ~0.2–2 Hz |
| `PHASE` | Continuous value = the source lane's own loop phase `stepIndex / laneLength`. Carries polymeter. | ≤ 2.5 Hz, capped |
| `DRIFT` | Slow value noise over 8–60 s, plus a step change on a mutation epoch. | ≤ 0.1 Hz |

`INTEGRATE` and `PHASE` are the two that make this not-a-visualiser: they produce continuous
quantities **that have no audio counterpart at that rate**. A listener cannot hear the hat lane's
32-step loop as a rhythm; the light can show it as a slowly rotating colour. That is genuinely new
information, not a copy of the audio.

## 3. AGGREGATE — the rule that prevents a flash train

**A ratchet of 4 emits four `SequencedEvent`s**, spaced a quarter-step apart, with `HitFlag::ratchet`
on sub-hits 2–4 and nothing on sub-hit 1 (`GrooveEngine.cpp:356-380`). Ghost notes add more events.
Live MIDI adds more. A light engine that responds per event turns a ratchet into a strobe.

**Rule [C]:** the aggregator groups events by `(voice, tickIndex, stepIndex)` within one sequencer
tick and emits **one** light event:

- amplitude = **max** of the group's velocities (not the sum — a sum rewards ratchets with brightness
  and re-creates the problem in the luminance domain);
- optionally a "stutter" flag the mapping may use to pick a *shape* (e.g. a triplet of slow humps at
  the mapping's own 2 Hz ceiling), never a faster one;
- the group's amplitude is then clamped, so a 4-ratchet and a single hit of the same velocity give
  the same light.

This requires hook **H5** (`ratchetIndex` / `ratchetCount` on the event) or, as a fallback, grouping
by `(voice, tickIndex, stepIndex)` which is already available.

## 4. The three mapping personalities

Each is a *complete* configuration, not a partial preset. The issue asks for "a few clear mapping
personalities with playable macros for coupling and intensity". These three differ in **what carries
the rhythm**: brightness (PULSE), colour and space (COUNTERPOINT), or time (DRIFT). They are
deliberately not three flavours of the same flash.

All numbers below are **[C]** starting points to be tuned by ear and by the tests in
[`05-acceptance-and-tests.md`](05-acceptance-and-tests.md). They are *defaults*, not truth.

### 4.1 PULSE — "the skeleton breathes"

**Thesis:** the kick and snare are the light's body; everything else is grain in its surface.
The mapping a first-time user should meet, and the one that proves the format works.

| Voice | Source | Mode | Colour (RGB) | Eye | A / R | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GROUND** | KICK 1.0, TOM 0.25 | EVENT | 1.00, 0.55, 0.15 (amber) | both | 60 / 700 ms | 0.70 |
| **ANSWER** | SNARE 1.0, CLAP 0.60 | EVENT | 0.15, 0.45, 1.00 (cool blue) | alternate | 45 / 420 ms | 0.50 |
| **GRAIN** | CH, OH, PERC, ODD, ghosts | INTEGRATE (τ 500 ms) | 0.60, 0.60, 0.75 (neutral) | both | — | 0.08 |

- GROUND and ANSWER overlap. The **amount of overlap**, not the event time, is what shows the groove
  (§6). At 120 BPM with a 700 ms release the GROUND level is continuous with a ripple tuned to stay
  under the 10 % luminance-flash threshold.
- GRAIN is capped at 8 % depth and integrates, so 16th hats at 200 BPM produce a *slowly shifting
  surface*, not 13 flashes a second. This is the direct answer to *"hats/percussie voor textuur"*.
- `alternate` on ANSWER puts the snare answer on the opposite eye from the last one: a call and
  response across the visual field that is present only when the pattern actually has a snare answer.

### 4.2 COUNTERPOINT — "the light runs against the kit"

**Thesis:** the light has its own metre. Colour and position carry the rhythm; brightness barely
moves. This is the mapping that could not exist as an audio effect.

| Voice | Source | Mode | Colour | Eye | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LANE A** | HATS lane | PHASE (`stepIndex/len`) | 0.10, 1.00, 0.35 (green) | both | 0.60 |
| **LANE B** | PERC lane | PHASE | 0.15, 0.35, 1.00 (blue) | both | 0.60 |
| **LANE C** | ODD lane | PHASE | 1.00, 0.20, 0.25 (red) | both | 0.60 |
| **ANCHOR** | KICK + SNARE | EVENT → *position only* | — | L/R wander | 0.40 |

- Because the lanes have **different lengths**, the three colour drivers drift against each other.
  A 16-step hat lane, a 7-step perc lane and a 5-step odd lane have an LCM of **560 sixteenth-note
  steps** — at 120 BPM, **70 seconds** (560 × 0.125 s). The hat lane alone repeats every 2 s and the
  other two every 0.875 s and 0.625 s; **the colour field repeats only every 70 s.** That is 35× the
  audio's fastest cycle, and it is the strongest single argument that this is a composition and not a
  visualisation.
  - Note the scaling honestly: a longer pattern (64 steps) or more lanes with mutually prime lengths
    pushes the period into minutes; short lanes at fast tempi pull it back toward tens of seconds.
    The claim is "far longer than the audio's own period", not a fixed duration.
- **Total luminance is normalised** across the three channels while they cross-fade, so hue motion
  costs no luminance flashes. Note the caveat: LEDs differ in luminance at equal drive, so this is
  approximate; the governor in §5 is the backstop.
- The **ANCHOR** voice is deliberately moved out of brightness and into **L/R position**: the kick
  walks the field left, the snare walks it right. At fast tempi the walk rate is divided
  (§1.2), so the spatial pulse never exceeds 2.5 Hz.
- PHASE drivers are rate-capped: `period = max(laneCycleTime, 0.4 s)`. A pathological 3-step lane at
  200 BPM would otherwise spin at 8.9 Hz.

### 4.3 DRIFT — "the light remembers"

**Thesis:** time is the expressive dimension. The light lags, holds, and answers late; mutation
changes its character rather than its events. The mapping the issue's *"het mag pulseren, zweven,
achterblijven en antwoorden"* was written for.

| Voice | Source | Mode | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MEMORY** | all voices, max-aggregated | EVENT with `delayMs = 140`, A 200 ms / R 1800 ms | The light trails the kit by a fixed, deliberate delay. Colour = current hue, depth 0.35. |
| **HUE** | — | DRIFT | Slow value noise, period 20–60 s, bounded to ±0.20 of the hue circle. |
| **TENSION** | raw event density | INTEGRATE (τ 6 s) | Maps long-term *activity* to a slow desaturation. Blank bars drain the colour; a busy passage refills it. |

- The **140 ms delay is the point**, exactly as the inherited composition used a 180 ms answer: light
  and sound are family, not a copy. It must be heard as intention, which is a perceptual question the
  hardware session has to answer (`06` §5 Q2).
- **Mutation:** hook H4 is required. When `mutationEpoch` changes, DRIFT selects a new base hue and a
  new drift direction, crossfaded over **≥ 1000 ms**. The rhythm does not change; the *character*
  does. This is the direct fulfilment of *"MUTATE/EVOLVE als veranderingen in het visuele patroon —
  niet noodzakelijk extra flashes."*
- Because MEMORY's release is 1.8 s, event density barely matters: a busier pattern gets *denser
  colour*, not more flashes.

## 5. The governor — six gains in, six safe gains out

Runs after accumulation, before the encoder. It is not a limiter in the audio sense; it is the
guarantee that no mapping — including a favourite preset, a broken parameter, or a corrupted state —
can produce unsafe or ugly light.

```
for each channel c in {R,G,B} × {L,R}:
    gain[c] = slewLimit (gain[c], minRiseMs = 20)          // signal + safety
Y[c]    = 0.21·R + 0.72·G + 0.07·B                         // luminance PROXY, per ear
                                                           // c = the slower of the two ears

// exact sliding-window flash accounting (see below), one ring per ear
if Y has reversed direction after moving >= 10% of the ceiling:
    if now - transitionTime[ring, N-1] < 1.0 s:            // N = 6
        freeze luminance this frame, keep hue and position moving
    else:
        pushTransition(now)
```

### 5.1 Definitions — write these into the code as comments

A token bucket is **not** sufficient, and an earlier draft of this document was wrong to propose one. A bucket of
3 refilling at 3/s allows 3 edges immediately and 3 more over the following second: 6 edges in one
sliding second, which is 3 flashes — but only if every pair happens to pair up. It does not enforce a
bound in *every* window. Use exact accounting.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Luminance proxy `Y`** | `0.21·R + 0.72·G + 0.07·B` per ear. An **estimate**, not a measurement. |
| **Transition** | A reversal of `dY/dt` after `Y` has moved ≥ **10 % of the current ceiling** since the previous extremum. |
| **Flash** | One opposing **pair** of transitions (WCAG 2.3.1's unit). One complete up-down or down-up cycle. |
| **Budget** | **≤ 3 flashes in every sliding 1-second window** ⇒ at most **6 transitions** in any sliding 1 s. |

**Implementation:** a 6-entry ring of sample counters per ear. A new transition is only admitted if the
oldest of the previous 6 is at least `sampleRate` samples in the past. That is O(1), allocation-free,
and exactly enforces the bound rather than approximating it. Six, not three, because a flash is a
*pair*: allowing 3 transitions would cap the light at 1.5 flashes/s, which is stricter than necessary
and would make the 2.5 Hz design pulse of §1.2 impossible.

**Consistency check with §1.2:** a 2.5 Hz luminance pulse is 2.5 flashes/s and 5 transitions/s. That is
inside a budget of 3 flashes / 6 transitions per second, with margin. The two numbers are compatible —
but only under the flash-pair definition above. If a builder counts transitions against a budget of 3,
the design violates its own governor.

### 5.2 The rest of the governor

- **Slew limit first.** 20 ms minimum edges are the safety floor (**[E]** safety rules) and also
  keep modulation sidebands inside the channel's own 500 Hz band (**[I]**, `01` §7).
- **Luminance weights are an inference [I], not a safety proof.** 0.21/0.72/0.07 are typical
  LED-luminance weights. The Kasina's actual per-colour luminance is **[O]**. **The governor bounds a
  computed proxy; it does not bound photons.** It cannot be presented as evidence that the physical
  output is flicker-free — only a photodiode measurement can ([`06`](06-hardware-validation.md) T6).
- **Freeze luminance, not colour.** When the budget is exhausted the light holds its brightness
  steady while hue keeps moving. This is deliberately the opposite of a mute: the light never blinks
  out to protect you, it *stops changing brightness and keeps being light*. It cannot be perceived as
  an error and it is trivially testable offline (§7 C4).
- **The governor is measurable.** Rendered offline, `Y(t)` can be counted for opposing ≥ 10 %
  transitions in **every** sliding 1 s window. That number is an acceptance criterion, not an
  intention. **Neither the inherited NAGLOED implementation nor any existing KLOPGEEST code implements
  this** — it is new work and it is the single most important safety component.
- **Adversarial boundary tests are mandatory**, because off-by-one errors in a sliding window are
  invisible in normal use: exactly 6 transitions in 1.000 s must pass; 6 transitions in 0.999 s must
  fail; a burst of 6 at t=0 followed by 1 at t=0.5 s must fail; a steady 3 Hz square envelope must
  pass; a steady 3.5 Hz square envelope must fail. See [`05`](05-acceptance-and-tests.md) §3.

## 6. How groove becomes visible without flashing

Swing and microtiming are 5–40 ms phenomena. A 700 ms light envelope cannot show a 20 ms deviation as
a time shift. Three mechanisms do show it, and all three are in the *design*, not the timing:

1. **Overlap.** Two light voices with overlapping envelopes: the deviation changes the **shape of the
   overlap** — which voice is ahead at the crossing point, and how deep the crossing is. At slow
   envelopes a 25 ms shift is a small but continuous change in a *shape*, which the eye integrates,
   rather than a discrete event the eye must time. This is why the timing must be preserved
   sample-accurately even though the light is slow: the *rendered result* depends on it.
2. **Transposition into space.** `timingDevMs` can bias the L/R position of a light voice. The human
   visual system is far more sensitive to a moving edge's position than to a flash's onset time.
   **[C]** Transpose time into space; never into strobe.
3. **Lag as content.** DRIFT's fixed delay is itself the visible groove: the light that arrives late
   is the statement.

**What must NOT be done:** shortening envelopes until microtiming is "visible" as flashes. That
converts a groove into a strobe and is precisely the failure the issue names.

## 7. When is it a rhythmic representation and not a visualisation?

Five operational criteria. The first four are offline tests a CI can run; the fifth needs people.

| # | Criterion | Test | Status today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1a** | **Light off is exactly the old instrument.** | Render with `lightEnabled = false`; the buffer must be **bit-identical** to a render from the pre-feature build. | Achievable by construction: the light stage returns before touching a sample. |
| **C1b** | **Light on does not change the music.** The signal path is the same instrument with a declared filter and a declared gain change. | The permitted differences are exactly two: the programme anti-alias/notch stage (magnitude-flat in the audible band) and the headroom attenuation of `01` §5 (≤ 1 dB at the product cap). Assert (i) every `SequencedEvent` is identical between light-off and light-on, and (ii) with the declared gain applied to the light-off render, the two agree to ≤ −80 dBFS in 0–16 kHz once the declared filter is also applied. | Achievable, and honest. **Do not promise bit-identity for C1b** — a notch shifts phase, and the gain change is real. |
| **C1c** | **The filter does its job and does nothing else.** | Measure the programme's spectrum before and after the anti-alias/notch stage. | Pass when 17–20 kHz is ≤ −40 dB re the carrier band **and** the 0–16 kHz magnitude response is flat within ±0.1 dB. |
| **C2** | **The light is rhythmic on its own.** | Render the carriers with the programme muted. The luminance envelope's autocorrelation must peak at the pattern period, and differ between two different factory patterns. A flat or non-periodic result is decoration. | **Not established.** Nothing built. |
| **C3** | **It uses the groove.** | Render at STABILITY 1.0 (machine) and 0.0 (wild). The variance of the light's *overlap shapes* / spatial deviation must differ measurably. If identical, the light is not representing the groove. | **Not established.** |
| **C4** | **It survives mutation without getting faster.** | Press MUTATE ten times. The light texture changes; the luminance edge rate stays inside the governor's budget. | **Not established.** Needs H4. |
| **C5** | **A person can tell.** | With sound off and only the light: can a viewer distinguish TIGHT from FERAL above chance? PULSE from COUNTERPOINT? | **Not established, and only a person can establish it.** This is the artistic bar and it is the *definition of done* that matters most. |

**The honest position:** C1a–C4 are engineering claims this research can specify and a builder can
test. C5 is the actual question of the issue — *is this light composing or decorating?* — and it is
**not answerable by research**. It requires a build and a Kasina in a room with a person in it. Any
claim that the mappings "work" before C5 is answered would be inference presented as finding.

## 8. Macros and parameters

Two macros, as the issue asks, plus the minimum switches. **[C]**, ranges proposed.

| Parameter | Range | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lightEnabled` | bool | **false** | Master opt-in. Off = not one sample of carrier is generated. |
| `lightMapping` | PULSE / COUNTERPOINT / DRIFT | PULSE | Which personality. |
| `lightIntensity` | 0.0–1.0 | 0.25 | Sets `r = rCap · intensity`. **1.0 means the cap (`r = 0.25`, 6.25 % of full brightness, −48.04 dBFS at ref −36), not 100 % of the format.** See the canonical units table in [`01`](01-signal-spec.md) §3. |
| `lightCoupling` | 0.0–1.0 | 0.7 | 0 = the light runs on its own slow clock, only loosely phase-locked to the bar. 1 = full event response. |
| `lightReferenceGainDb` | −48…−24 dB | −36.0 | Format trim ([`01`](01-signal-spec.md) §3). |
| `lightColorGainDb` | −36…−12 dB | −24.0 | Format trim. |
| `lightPanic` | momentary | — | Kills colours then reference within 50 ms ([`04`](04-safety-and-opt-in.md) §4). |

**INTENSITY is not a volume knob and must not be presented as one.** It is a *risk* control: it
scales the maximum per-carrier amplitude. Its label, its tooltip and its behaviour should make that
unmistakable. Its maximum (1.0 → `r = 0.25` → **6.25 % of full brightness**) is itself a conservative
research convention, not a hardware limit **[O]**.

**COUPLING is the musical macro.** At 0 the light is a slow, self-running composition in the same
key as the music; at 1 it answers the kit. The interesting values are in between, which is why it is
a continuous macro rather than a switch.

### What is deliberately NOT a parameter

- Full RGB pickers. The issue asks for "clear mapping personalities", not a lighting desk.
- Per-voice light depth. Eight faders would turn the instrument into a mixer.
- Flash, strobe or "rate" controls. There is no safe way to offer them and no artistic need.
- A "make it brighter than the ceiling" escape. The ceiling is the ceiling.

## 9. Design consequences of the hardware evidence

The one real hardware session (a Kasina + DeepVision, colours at `r = 0.0625` — 6.25 % of the
reference amplitude, 1.56 % of full brightness, −54.1 dBFS absolute) reported **red, blue and violet plus left/right asymmetry, but no convincing green or yellow**, and
crossfades that read as black. This must shape the design, not be explained away:

1. **Do not encode meaning in colour identity.** A mapping whose message is "green = the hats, red =
   the kick" fails if green is dim on this hardware. PULSE and COUNTERPOINT above use colour as a
   *continuum* and a *mixing field*; the meaning lives in brightness, overlap and position.
2. **Never cross-fade to black.** The observed black transitions may be a level threshold, a
   sublinear decoder curve, or adaptation. Whatever the cause, the design rule is the same: **make
   before break** — the incoming colour must reach its level before the outgoing one begins to fall,
   with a documented overlap. Cheap, and it removes an entire class of complaint.
3. **Green may need its own gain.** Per-colour trim is already in the vendor's own encoder. If the
   hardware ladder confirms a green deficit, add a per-colour balance parameter — do not re-architect.
4. **The dim floor is unknown [O].** The product range spans `f` = 0 to 6.25 % of full brightness;
   the observed range was 1.56 %. Both may sit under the decoder's useful floor. That is exactly what
   the ladder in [`06`](06-hardware-validation.md) exists to find. Until it is run, **the default is
   conservative and the documentation says the light may be dim.**

## 10. What to build first

A staged plan that keeps the risk visible at every step. **No stage is authorised by this document.**

| Stage | Deliverable | Proves |
|---|---|---|
| **L0** | Encoder only, no mappings. A fixed test pattern drives the six channels. Offline WAV via `klop-tool`. | Signal integrity: frequencies, levels, isolation, headroom, determinism. |
| **L1** | Governor + PULSE. Offline render and measurement of the luminance edge rate. | C1a–C1c, and that the safety budget is measurable and enforced. |
| **L2** | AGGREGATE + COUNTERPOINT + DRIFT. `mutationEpoch` (H4). | C2, C3, C4. |
| **L3** | Hardware ladder on a real Kasina (`06`). | Detection, usable brightness range, colour identity, stop behaviour. |
| **L4** | A person, the mappings, the five questions in `06` §5. | **C5 — the only one that answers the issue.** |

Stages L0–L2 are implementable and testable without hardware. L3–L4 are not, and no claim about them
can be made until they happen.
